Patterns across the two quarters

  • Robbery-type crime is still falling, but momentum is fading fast. House robbery's year-on-year decline more than halved (−17.6% → −7.0%), business robbery's nearly halved (−11.8% → −7.0%), and even carjacking — the best-performing category in both quarters — eased slightly (−18.2% → −16.8%). If this deceleration continues, these categories could turn positive within a quarter or two.
  • Murder and attempted murder tell a similar story. Murder's decline slowed from −5.9% to −3.5%, while attempted murder kept rising but at a much gentler rate (+8.7% → +3.0%). Read together, violent crime's improvement is losing steam across the board, not just in property-related categories.
  • Sexual offences are the one category moving the other way. Both sexual offences overall and rape specifically went from nearly flat in Q1 (−0.8%, −0.3%) to a clearer year-on-year decline in Q2 (−2.7%, −2.4%) — the only tracked category where the trend improved between quarters.
  • The national headline number is hiding the divergence underneath. Overall contact crime declined at an almost identical −2.3% in both quarters — a reassuringly stable top-line figure — but that stability masks robbery-type crime's fading improvement being offset by sexual offences' newly accelerating decline.
  • Cash-in-transit heists spiked 50% (24 → 36 incidents) in Q2, with no comparable Q1 figure available to confirm whether this is a new trend or continues one already underway — worth watching next quarter given how quickly this category can swing off a small base.
  • Murder remains geographically uneven. The Eastern Cape, Free State and Northern Cape posted murder increases in both the Q1 and Q2 releases, even as the national murder count falls — flagged by the Acting Police Minister as a persistent concern.
