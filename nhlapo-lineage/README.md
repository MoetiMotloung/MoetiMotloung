# Nhlapo (Nhlapho / Hlapo) and the Bhele — Lineage Notes

Research notes on the **Nhlapo** lineage, on how the **amaBhele** ("Belo", "Mabele") came to be in
Lesotho, and on where the rest of them live today.

> **Short answer:** the Bhele are a Nguni people from **Lenge, south of Ladysmith (KwaZulu-Natal)**.
> The wars of the **Mfecane/Lifaqane (c. 1818–1830s)** scattered them. Some crossed the Drakensberg
> and were taken in by **Moshoeshoe I**, and that is how a Bhele (and Hlubi/Zizi) element became part
> of the Basotho. Most of the others went south into the **Eastern Cape**, where they were counted among
> the **amaMfengu**, or stayed in **KwaZulu-Natal**. Some are in **Eswatini**.
>
> **On Nhlapo:** the written sources I could reach **do not show that the Nhlapo are a Bhele clan**.
> The Nhlapho praises are their own and don't overlap with the Mbhele praises. In Lesotho the Nhlapho are
> recorded as one of the **Nguni groups tied to the Hlubi**, with a chief named **Mlambo** in the
> Butha-Buthe area. If your family says it is "Nhlapo of the Bhele", that may be true, but it is family
> tradition the sources here don't confirm or rule out (see §5).

---

## 1. Who the Bhele are

| | |
|---|---|
| **Names** | amaBhele, Bhele. In Sesotho: *Mabele*, and like other Nguni incomers, often called *Matebele*. Surname form: **Mbhele**. |
| **Origin** | **Lenge**, about 20 km south of Ladysmith, KwaZulu-Natal, where their ancestor **Bhele** is said to have lived about 250 years ago. |
| **Family of peoples** | Nguni, close to the **amaHlubi** and **amaZizi**. During the wars the Hlubi joined the Bhele on the Lenge mountains "to better resist the attacks of Matiwane" (Maxengana). |
| **Clan praises (Mbhele)** | *uLanga, uKhuboni, uNtshangase, uMaphekwa* (popular collections; versions vary). |
| **Organisation today** | **amaBhele AseLenge**, which promotes and preserves Bhele history and identity. Its account has been debated with academic historians (UCT, 2012). |

## 2. How the Bhele came to Lesotho

| Approx. date | Event |
|---|---|
| before c. 1818 | Bhele living around Lenge, near the Hlubi (upper Mzinyathi / Thukela area). |
| c. 1818–1822 | The Mfecane begins. **Matiwane's amaNgwane**, pushed by the Ndwandwe and Zulu, attack the Hlubi and their neighbours. The Hlubi and Bhele band together at Lenge. |
| early 1820s | The Bhele break up and scatter. One stream goes **west over the Drakensberg** onto the Caledon / Highveld, the same route taken by Hlubi (under Mpangazitha) and Ngwane (under Matiwane). This is the start of the **Lifaqane** for the Basotho. |
| 1820s | Moshoeshoe I moves from **Butha-Buthe** to **Thaba Bosiu** (1824) and builds a kingdom by **taking in refugees**, both Sotho clans and Nguni groups. Bhele, Hlubi and Zizi families settle under him and his sons, many of them in the **north (Butha-Buthe, Leribe)** and in the mountains. |
| later 19th c. | Nguni families gradually take on Sesotho language and names (for example *Nhlapo → Hlapo*, *Mbhele → Mabele*), and they are counted as Basotho. The Morija Museum's Hlubi research notes that these Nguni groups "have not received very much attention" in Lesotho's history, and that many now know their clan but not its tie to the wider nation. |

**Why there is little written detail:** Ellenberger's *History of the Basuto* (1912), the standard account,
follows the Sotho chiefly houses closely and covers the Nguni incomers mostly as invaders (Ngwane, Hlubi).
I found no named Bhele chief in Lesotho in the sources I could reach.

## 3. Where the rest of the Bhele are

| Branch | Where today | How they are identified |
|---|---|---|
| **Stayed in / returned to Natal** | **KwaZulu-Natal**: Ladysmith/Lenge area, also Zululand (one section under **Sompisi kaGuqa** settled there, per a less reliable online source) | Mbhele surname; amaBhele AseLenge |
| **Went south — "amaMfengu"** | **Eastern Cape**: Peddie, Butterworth, Tsomo, Ngqamakhwe, Keiskammahoek and other Mfengu areas. *Mfengu* became the umbrella name for Hlubi, Bhele, Zizi and others who fled Natal. | Speak isiXhosa; Mbhele / Bhele clan names |
| **Went west — Lesotho** | **Lesotho** (especially the north) and the neighbouring **Free State** | Speak Sesotho; Sesotho forms of Nguni surnames |
| **Eswatini** | Eswatini | Siswati; listed among the Bhele's descendants |

## 4. The Nhlapo / Nhlapho line

| | |
|---|---|
| **Spellings** | Nhlapo, Nhlapho; in Lesotho often **Hlapo**. |
| **Clan praises (izithakazelo)** | *Sgegede, Mwelase, Nongombili, Mbonane* … and a river line, *wena mlambo ka-Mavundla*. Several versions circulate (credited to *Izithakazelo Nezibongo ZakwaZulu*), so families differ. |
| **Where found** | KwaZulu-Natal, **Mpumalanga** (for example the Ermelo area, from family accounts), Gauteng, **Eswatini**, and **Lesotho**. Family accounts online say the Nhlapo "came from Natal". |
| **In Lesotho** | The **Morija Museum & Archives Hlubi project** interviewed **Mzamane Nhlapho** of **Butha-Buthe**. He recites the Nhlapho clan name and recalls *"a Nhlapho Chief who was installed in those days known as Mlambo."* The project's final report lists the Nhlapo among the groups who want their place in the **Hlubi** story recognised. |

## 5. Bhele or Hlubi? Weighing the evidence

| Points toward a **Bhele** link | Points toward a **Hlubi** (or Swazi) link |
|---|---|
| Same homeland area (Lenge / upper Thukela) and the same flight into Lesotho | The Lesotho oral-history project files the Nhlapho under the **Hlubi** |
| The Bhele and Hlubi fought side by side, and families mixed | The Nhlapho praises (*Mwelase, Nongombili …*) share no names with the Mbhele praises (*Langa, Khuboni …*) |
| Your family may remember it as Bhele | Strong Nhlapho presence in **Mpumalanga/Eswatini** suggests Swazi-area ties for some lines |

**Verdict:** the Nhlapo are a **Natal Nguni clan that came into Lesotho in the Lifaqane alongside the Bhele and Hlubi**.
That much is well supported. Whether your Nhlapo line is **Bhele** in the strict sense (a house of Bhele's
descendants) is **unproven**. The nearest documented tie in Lesotho is to the **Hlubi**.

## 6. How to trace your own line

1. **Write down your family's full izithakazelo / seboko.** If it includes *Mwelase, Sgegede, Nongombili*, you're in the
   main Nhlapho line. If it includes *Langa, Khuboni, Ntshangase*, it's the Mbhele/Bhele line.
2. **Find the chief your family lived under.** In Butha-Buthe, ask about the **Mlambo** Nhlapho chieftaincy and the local
   chief's office.
3. **Morija Museum & Archives** (Morija, Lesotho): ask for the **Hlubi research project** interviews, including
   *Mzamane Nhlapho*, and the final report. Copies are online in UCT's **Five Hundred Year Archive / Emandulo**
   (Hlubi collection, Morija sub-collection).
4. **amaBhele AseLenge** (KwaZulu-Natal) for the Bhele side, and the Mfengu histories for the Eastern Cape branches.
5. **Written sources:**
   - D. F. Ellenberger & J. C. Macgregor, *History of the Basuto, Ancient and Modern* (1912) — chapters on the Lifaqane
   - J. Ayliff & J. Whiteside, *History of the Abambo, generally known as Fingos* (1912)
   - N. Maxengana, *The History and Historiography of the amaMfengu* (UCT thesis)
   - R. T. Kawa, *Ibali lamaMfengu* (1929)
   - H. Patrick, *Tibongo Netinanatelo TemaSwati* (Swazi surnames and praise names) — for the Swazi-side Nhlapho
