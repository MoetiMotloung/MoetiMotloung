Overall mortality profile

What share of all deaths each year come from non‑communicable diseases versus infectious, maternal, nutritional and injury causes combined — the clearest single marker of where each country sits on the "epidemiological transition."

Infectious disease burden

South Africa carries the world's largest HIV epidemic and one of the highest TB rates. Mozambique and Angola face the heaviest combined HIV+malaria load. Tanzania's burden is dominated by malaria. Each panel uses its own scale — units differ across panels.

Solutions & interventions

What is actively being deployed against each disease group across the four countries, and where the region's biggest structural risk now sits.

!

Funding shock, 2025. A US foreign-aid freeze disrupted PEPFAR-funded HIV prevention, testing and treatment support across the region just as the Global Fund's Eighth Replenishment fell short — raising $11.4B of an $18B target at its November 2025 summit in Johannesburg. Modelling projects up to 74,000 excess HIV deaths across Africa by 2030 if disruptions persist. Dependency on that funding is very uneven across these four countries — see Regional balance below.

HIV — treat and prevent at scale

Universal test-and-treat with same-day antiretroviral therapy (ART) start; pre-exposure prophylaxis (PrEP, including long-acting injectable options); voluntary medical male circumcision; and the DREAMS programme for adolescent girls and young women. South Africa runs the world's largest ART programme (~5.9M people on treatment) and is now the most donor-independent of the four; Mozambique and Tanzania still rely on PEPFAR for the bulk of prevention funding, which halted DREAMS activities in 2025.

TB — find, treat, prevent

Rapid molecular diagnostics (GeneXpert), community-based active case-finding, TB preventive therapy for people living with HIV, and household contact tracing. Mozambique's community-based service delivery model and Tanzania are both on track to meet the WHO End TB 2025 milestone (75% reduction in deaths vs. 2015); South Africa and Angola — the two highest-incidence countries here — are scaling shorter multidrug-resistant TB regimens and digital chest X-ray screening.

Malaria — nets, spraying, and now vaccines

Mass distribution of insecticide-treated nets (including newer dual-active-ingredient nets to counter resistance), indoor residual spraying, and seasonal chemoprevention. Mozambique introduced the RTS,S/R21 malaria vaccine into routine immunisation in 2024; Tanzania and Angola have not yet introduced a vaccine (Angola has plans underway). South Africa — near elimination — instead runs targeted spraying and cross-border coordination with Mozambique and Eswatini (the Lubombo/Trans-frontier malaria initiative) to stop imported cases from re-establishing transmission.

NCDs — primary-care integration

WHO's PEN and PEN-Plus packages push hypertension and diabetes screening and treatment into primary care and severe-case management (type 1 diabetes, rheumatic heart disease) into first-level rural hospitals. South Africa has the region's most mature response — a sugar-sweetened beverage levy and salt-reduction regulation alongside PEN-Plus pilots — while Tanzania, Mozambique and Angola are earlier in rolling out NCD surveillance (STEPS surveys) and integrating chronic care, with WHO AFRO reporting NCD deaths in the region have risen from 24% (2000) to 37% of all deaths (2019).

Regional balance: who depends on what

Share of national HIV programme funding coming from the single largest external donor (PEPFAR) — the clearest fault line in how resilient each country's response is to the 2025 funding shock.

South Africa: most self-financed, still highest combined burden. Under 25% PEPFAR-dependent and able to absorb funding shocks better, but it still carries the region's largest HIV epidemic, highest TB incidence, and — as the most economically developed of the four — the most advanced (and largest) NCD burden.
Tanzania & Mozambique: highest exposure to the funding shock. Roughly 90% and 82% of HIV prevention funding respectively comes from PEPFAR, so the 2025 disruption hit hardest here — even though both have made genuine gains (Mozambique's malaria vaccine rollout, both nations' near-met End TB milestones).
Angola: least data, real disruption. National-level survey data on NCDs and even HIV prevention financing is sparser for Angola than the other three — a gap in itself — but PEPFAR-funded outreach has already stopped in 4 of its 21 provinces in 2025, and it remains one of the ten highest malaria-case countries worldwide.
One region, four different transition points. Mozambique and Angola still sit earliest in the communicable-to-NCD transition (NCDs ~28–32% of deaths); Tanzania is mid-transition (~34%); South Africa is furthest along (~51%) — meaning the same regional funding cut lands on four very different underlying risk profiles at once.
